Fonts

Typeface or Font

A typeface (or type family) is the name of a specific collection of related fonts. In comparison, font refers to a particular weight, width, and style within that typeface. To put it in simple terms, each variation of a typeface is a font.

Lots of people say “font” when they mean “typeface.” We welcome them and we don’t think it’s pedantic at all. Wish us luck!

Garamond

Garamond is a group of many serif typefaces, named for sixteenth-century Parisian engraver Claude Garamond, generally spelled as Garamont in his lifetime. Garamond-style typefaces are popular and particularly often used for book printing and body text.

Univers

The OG Helvetica. Univers was one of the first typeface families to fulfil the idea that a typeface should form a family of consistent, related designs.
